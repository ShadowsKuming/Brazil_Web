## Background & Context

A key challenge in photography education lies in the limited pedagogical support available to marginalised learners and novices for developing reflective and intentional creative thinking. Existing photography education is largely organised around two dominant instructional approaches. The first is theory-driven, academy-based instruction, in which aesthetic standards and interpretive frameworks are established through art appreciation prior to making. The second is experience-driven instruction, which emphasises repeated practice followed by expert feedback to incrementally improve visual outcomes. 

While both approaches are well established in professional education, they rely heavily on sustained access to specialised teachers, time, and material resources. As a result, they are difficult to maintain in resource-constrained or intermittently supported learning environments. More critically, each approach embeds an early pedagogical commitment that shapes how creative learning unfolds. Theory-driven instruction often fixes expressive frameworks before learners have developed a clear sense of intent, limiting early exploration of meaning and theme. In contrast, experience-driven instruction postpones reflective thinking about what to express and why until after production, encouraging trial-and-error and post hoc correction without a stable creative purpose. In contexts where continuous guidance is unavailable, this structural imbalance can hinder the development of self-directed and sustained creative thinking.

This project addresses this gap by exploring a **pedagogical restructuring** of photography learning that foregrounds thinking without prescribing modes of expression. Rather than treating thematic intent and expressive exploration as sequential or fixed instructional stages, the project makes their relationship explicit and dynamically adjustable throughout the creative process. This negotiation is treated not as a one-off instructional decision but as a structural feature that shapes learning over time. Within this framework, AI-supported tools serve as one possible pathway for modulating the relative emphasis of different pedagogical functions during learning.

Beyond photography education, the pedagogical restructuring examined in this project is intended to be transferable. By articulating how instructional functions can be decomposed and re-coupled in response to learning processes, the project offers a broader design perspective for addressing pedagogical rigidity in other domains and for supporting multiple, evolving learning trajectories.

## Research Questions & Hypothesis

**RQ1.** How does a teaching structure that supports dynamic adjustment between thematic thinking and expressive exploration shape learners’ understanding of the creative process?

**RQ2.** How does such a structure influence learners’ reflective practice over time?

**Hypothesis**  
Teaching structures that support dynamic balancing between thematic thinking and expressive exploration are more likely to foster an integrated understanding of creative learning, in which learners experience the process as ongoing inquiry rather than isolated technical refinement.

## Approach & Infrastructure

### Research Approach
The project adopts a process-centred research approach that combines instructional design experiments with qualitative analysis. Two contrasting instructional configurations are examined. One follows conventional arrangements that emphasise theoretical instruction prior to practice or feedback after production. The other implements a teaching structure that supports continuous adjustment between thematic thinking and expressive exploration throughout the learning process.

By comparing learners’ creative processes, reflective accounts, and artefact development across these configurations, the study analyses how balancing structures evolve and how they shape learners’ experiences of creative learning.

### Infrastructure
- **AI-supported tools:**  
  Cloud or locally deployable computing resources enabling the use of open-source models or accessible large language model APIs, as appropriate.
- **Creative capture:**  
  Standard digital cameras for photography learning activities.
- **Output and reflection:**  
  Basic visual display tools, such as screens or printers, to support viewing, comparison, and reflective discussion of photographic work.

## Expected Outcomes

- A research prototype exploring how pedagogical restructuring can be instantiated in photography-based creative learning, serving as a vehicle for studying dynamic balancing between thematic thinking and expressive exploration.
- A peer-reviewed publication articulating pedagogical restructuring as a structural perspective on creative learning, with photography used as an illustrative case rather than a limiting application domain.
- Empirical insights into how different teaching structures shape learners’ understanding of the creative process and their reflective practices, informed by process-oriented analysis.
