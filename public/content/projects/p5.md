## Background & Context

Marginalised children often have limited access to art appreciation experiences that support careful looking, evidence-based interpretation, emotional articulation, and a sense of cultural belonging. In resource-constrained primary schools, this gap is reinforced by shortages of specialist art teachers, limited culturally relevant materials, and a lack of practical classroom methods for facilitating interpretive discussion. As a result, art learning is frequently reduced to copying techniques or isolated craft activities, rather than reflective meaning-making.

At the same time, many educational technologies assume high infrastructure and high literacy. For younger children—particularly those aged 7–8—screen-first interaction can introduce additional barriers. Attention is embodied, expression is often non-verbal, and explaining ideas is easier when children can point, move, and build. What is missing is an approach that makes art interpretation hands-on, playful, and teacher-facilitated, while remaining feasible in classrooms with limited resources.

This project proposes **Tangible Plot & Palette**, a tangible storytelling system that transforms children’s physical colour constructions into a bridge between feeling, interpretation, and artworks. The system is organised around an **aesthetic inquiry loop** comprising three components:  
1. Palette extraction from tangible builds;  
2. An affective mirror that offers mood cues as suggestions rather than judgements;  
3. Diversity-aware artwork recommendation from curated collections to prompt comparison, dialogue, and culturally grounded storytelling.

Within this framework, storytelling functions as a child-friendly interpretive scaffold. Children assemble simple narrative elements—such as who, where, what happened, and why it feels this way—to express an interpretation. Generated story prompts are explicitly framed as *contestable narrative hypotheses*, encouraging children to revise their interpretations by pointing to visual evidence in the artwork (e.g., “I think this because I see…”). This **contestable AI** stance supports teacher-led inquiry even in the absence of specialist training and avoids positioning the system as an interpretive authority.

The system is designed to fit classroom realities through a teacher-facilitated workflow using a laptop-based interface (camera input and curated content packs). Where appropriate, lightweight generative components can enrich engagement: large language models may generate short, age-appropriate story prompts framed as one possible interpretation, and image synthesis can be used as an optional creative reflection step that transforms a child’s block composition into a stylised image. In all cases, the interpretive focus remains on looking, comparing, and justifying meaning rather than on generation itself.

To strengthen generalisability and equity relevance, the project is co-designed and evaluated across Brazil and China, including both relatively resourced and under-resourced schools. This dual-region design examines whether the same tangible storytelling logic can adapt to different classroom ecologies and cultural repertoires without treating any single context as the default. Brazil, in particular, provides a high-impact pathway for collaboration, enabling educators to shape locally meaningful artwork sets and narrative prompts rooted in cultural heritage, regional stories, and everyday visual culture.

## Research Questions & Hypothesis

**RQ1.** How can tangible, narrative-based interaction—supported by palette-based affective mirroring and diversity-aware artwork recommendation—scaffold art appreciation and emotional expression for 7–8-year-old children in resource-constrained learning environments?

**RQ2.** To what extent does Tangible Plot & Palette improve children’s art appreciation outcomes, particularly interpretive depth and evidence-based reasoning, compared to standard classroom practice, and are these benefits larger in under-resourced settings?

**RQ3.** As a secondary effect, does improved interpretation support more coherent and grounded response artworks, and how do teachers appropriate the storytelling workflow in practice?

**Hypothesis**  
A tangible system that translates children’s colour constructions into mood cues, diverse artwork prompts, and contestable story hypotheses can strengthen engagement, emotional expression, and interpretive depth by making art inquiry embodied, playful, culturally adaptable, and easier for under-resourced teachers to facilitate. Gains are expected to be most pronounced where existing support for art appreciation is weakest.

## Approach & Infrastructure

### Research Approach
The project follows a teacher-led, cross-cultural co-design pathway in Brazil and China:

- **Formative teacher inquiry:**  
  Semi-structured interviews with primary school art teachers in both resourced and under-resourced schools to map constraints, lesson goals, assessment practices, and culturally meaningful themes.
- **Expert co-design and content authoring:**  
  Co-design sessions with art educators and invited experts to define lesson goals, curate artwork collections, and develop child-friendly representations of mood cues and narrative structures.
- **Iterative prototyping and field evaluation:**  
  Rapid prototyping combined with pre-deployment workshops, followed by comparative classroom studies in both countries.

Evaluation focuses on:
- **Art appreciation:**  
  Children’s verbal interpretations scored using a three-level rubric (description → inference → evidence-linked interpretation).
- **Art creation (secondary):**  
  Blind expert review of response artworks, emphasising interpretive grounding and narrative coherence.
- **Mixed methods:**  
  Child-friendly Likert scales (engagement and confidence), classroom observation, and teacher interviews or journals.

### Research Infrastructure
- Low-cost tangible kit (coloured blocks, narrative pieces, and build mat)
- Laptop-based capture and facilitation interface (camera input and interaction logging)
- Palette extraction and affective mirroring module (suggestions, not judgements)
- Curated artwork datasets for Brazil and China with diversity-aware recommendation
- Optional generative modules (LLM-based story prompts and diffusion-style image synthesis), governed by teacher-selected lesson packs
- Qualitative and quantitative data collection tools (observations, interviews, child-friendly scales, artefact capture)

## Expected Outcomes

- A deployable Tangible Plot & Palette prototype refined through co-design and field validation across Brazil and China, accompanied by a facilitation guide for under-resourced art teachers.
- Empirical evidence on palette-driven affective mirroring, diversity-aware recommendation, and contestable story prompts as scaffolds for children’s art appreciation, with secondary evidence on interpretation-grounded response making.
- Design principles and an open-access toolkit—including co-design protocols, content curation workflows, evaluation rubrics, and deployment manuals—for equitable art appreciation systems in resource-constrained learning environments.
