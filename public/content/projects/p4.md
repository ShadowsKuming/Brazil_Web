## Background & Context

Educational games are widely recognised for their potential to increase learning motivation and engagement. However, marginalised children and adolescents often face significant barriers to accessing these benefits. Many AI-assisted educational games rely heavily on direct manipulation interfaces—such as dragging, clicking, or visual experimentation—which, while appearing user-friendly, frequently encourage low-cost trial-and-error behaviours. In such environments, learners may bypass deliberate reasoning in favour of repeated guessing, undermining the core educational objective.

This interactional mismatch can be particularly exclusionary for marginalised learners. When educational progress is achievable through physical manipulation rather than articulated reasoning, differences in access to technology, digital familiarity, and confidence are amplified, further widening educational inequalities. In addition, many AI-enabled educational games lack accessibility-focused design, failing to accommodate diverse linguistic expressions, communication styles, and levels of digital literacy among marginalised children.

To address this gap, this project proposes **text-based interaction** as an inclusive alternative interaction paradigm. The goal is not to simplify interfaces, but to shift the locus of interaction toward the articulation of thinking. By requiring learners to express their reasoning in natural language, text-based interaction foregrounds conscious rule construction and reflective engagement.

The project operationalises this approach through a **“text-as-command”** interaction model, initially applied to teaching foundational programming concepts. Rather than manipulating visual elements directly, learners must describe the underlying logic driving the game state (e.g., “if the box is zero, move it to the outbox”). This design ensures that learners engage with the principles of the task, rather than relying on visual trial-and-error strategies. While the current prototype focuses on algorithmic thinking, the underlying mechanism—where language-based reasoning directly drives system behaviour—is designed to be flexible and transferable to other subject domains.

Despite its potential, text-based interaction remains underexplored in research on AI-assisted educational games, representing a missed opportunity for equitable learning design. This project addresses this gap through empirical investigation and cross-cultural collaboration between the UK and Brazil, ensuring that the proposed designs are culturally relevant and responsive to the lived realities of marginalised Brazilian children.

## Research Question & Hypotheses

**Research Question**  
How does text-based interaction in AI-assisted educational games influence learning outcomes, and what design principles can support the development of inclusive games for diverse learner populations?

**Hypotheses**  
1. Text-based interaction in AI-assisted educational games leads to improved learning outcomes compared to direct manipulation interfaces.  
2. AI-driven natural language understanding enhances inclusivity by reducing technical barriers for learners with varied digital backgrounds and linguistic expressions.

---

## Approach & Infrastructure

### Research Approach
The study employs an empirical research design involving two iterations of AI-assisted educational games. In the text-based condition, learners articulate their reasoning in natural language, receive AI-interpreted adaptive feedback, and progress through subsequent challenges. A direct manipulation condition serves as a comparison baseline.

Pre-study user research will be conducted to inform target demographics, content design, and interaction formats. Comparative evaluation will examine learning outcomes, cognitive engagement, and accessibility across conditions using both quantitative and qualitative methods.

### Research Infrastructure
- **Natural Language Understanding:**  
  An AI module capable of interpreting diverse linguistic expressions and mapping them to game logic.
- **Evaluation Framework:**  
  A mixed-methods comparative evaluation framework combining behavioural metrics with qualitative analysis.

---

## Expected Outcomes

- A peer-reviewed publication presenting empirical insights into the effects of text-based interaction on learning outcomes, cognitive engagement, and accessibility.
- Evidence-based design principles for inclusive, child-centred AI-assisted educational games, hosted on an open-access platform.
- A functional prototype of an AI-assisted educational game employing text-based interaction, accompanied by technical documentation and a user guide.
