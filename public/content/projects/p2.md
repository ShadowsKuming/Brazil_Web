## Background & Context

Digital learning technologies frequently overlook the interaction styles, cultural contexts, and infrastructural constraints of marginalised children. Many systems implicitly assume stable internet access, prior academic knowledge, and a baseline level of digital literacy—assumptions that do not hold in resource-constrained Brazilian contexts. As a result, these technologies often fail to engage the very learners they aim to support.

Existing AI-driven educational tools commonly rely on intrusive data collection practices and ability-based labelling, raising significant privacy concerns while reinforcing marginalisation through oversimplified categorisation. Few approaches prioritise understanding *how* learners interact with educational content, or translating granular interaction patterns into adaptive learning support.

This project addresses this gap by advocating a shift from learner profiling and test-based scoring toward **privacy-conscious, fine-grained analysis of interaction behaviour**. Through a UK–Brazil collaborative research framework, the project develops an interaction analysis approach to inform the design of inclusive, child-centred AI educational tools that respond to diverse interaction patterns while respecting contextual and ethical constraints.

---

## Research Questions & Hypothesis

**Research Question**  
How can interactive learning content, combined with granular interaction analysis, support the design of inclusive AI tools and adaptive learning mechanisms for marginalised children in Brazil?

**Hypothesis**  
Observable interaction signals—such as learning pace, pause duration, attention shifts, and sustained engagement—can provide actionable insights for designing adaptive, child-centred AI systems without relying on test-based assessment, learner profiling, or psychological labelling. Leveraging these signals can enhance accessibility and learning efficacy for marginalised learners while preserving privacy and agency.

---

## Approach & Infrastructure

**Collaborative Research Design**  
Researchers from the UK and Brazil will jointly design empirical studies and child-centred co-design activities, ensuring sensitivity to local educational practices, infrastructural realities, and ethical considerations.

**Interaction Data Infrastructure**  
The project utilises the extended **Kinetic Lab**, an interaction behaviour recording platform, to deliver short-form interactive learning materials and capture detailed interaction data. The infrastructure is designed to operate under low-resource conditions and intermittent connectivity, supported by a cross-national collaboration framework for secure and responsible data sharing.

---

## Expected Outcomes

- A child-centred interaction analysis framework for inclusive AI design, published as a peer-reviewed research contribution, explicitly avoiding inference about learners’ psychological states.
- Evidence-based design guidelines for inclusive, interaction-aware AI educational tools, hosted in an open-access repository.
- A functional prototype demonstrating how interaction-aware AI systems can provide adaptive learning support, accompanied by comprehensive technical documentation.
