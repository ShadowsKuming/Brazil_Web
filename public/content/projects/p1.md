
## Background & Context

Significant educational inequalities persist in marginalised regions of Brazil, where children often lack access to quality tutoring, locally relevant examples, and psychologically safe ways to ask for help. A key gap lies in the absence of pedagogical tools that actively cultivate reflective learning and proactive help-seeking, rather than merely delivering answers. These challenges are further amplified by intermittent connectivity and low or early-stage literacy.

Many existing AI tutoring systems implicitly assume stable internet access, high text literacy, and seamless real-time interaction. In Brazil’s sociotechnical reality, these assumptions frequently break down. WhatsApp is nearly ubiquitous, and voice notes (“audiozinhos”) are a dominant everyday communication practice—particularly for children who rely on voice before developing fluent typing skills. Asynchronous voice interaction also creates a pause-and-reflect space (replay, think, re-record), reducing performance pressure and supporting confidence building.

FreireVoice addresses this gap by combining two complementary strengths:

1. **Freirean problem-posing dialogue**, shifting AI from answer delivery toward reflective facilitation.
2. **Voice-first, asynchronous, seamful WhatsApp interaction**, which embraces infrastructural constraints and makes limitations visible, turning breakdowns into teachable moments and trust-building seams.

Educators and NGO mentors curate weekly learning packs (local examples, common misconceptions, and follow-up prompts) and oversee handoff seams through a lightweight dashboard, ensuring cultural relevance, pedagogical validity, and safety in real-world deployment.

## Research Questions

**RQ1.** To what extent can a co-designed, asynchronous voice-based WhatsApp assistant grounded in Freirean problem-posing dialogue enhance marginalised children’s help-seeking willingness, self-efficacy, reflective behaviours, and engagement, compared to  
(a) generic tutoring prompts and  
(b) answer-based chatbots?

**RQ2.** What conversational breakdowns and repair patterns emerge in asynchronous voice tutoring, and how does designing *repair-aware* interaction affect learner autonomy and confidence?

**RQ3.** In a teacher-in-the-loop architecture, how do different governance granularities—  
(a) weekly learning-pack authoring and review, and  
(b) trigger-based handoff seams for uncertain or sensitive cases—  
shape trust, perceived legitimacy, and educator workload?

**RQ4.** What forms of AI–education coupling are feasible, legitimate, and sustainable in low-resource, low-connectivity contexts, and how do local practices and constraints shape the design space of AI-powered educational systems?


## Approach & Infrastructure

**Co-design Approach**  
The project adopts a participatory co-design process with local educators and marginalised children to align FreireVoice with lived educational practices, cultural contexts, and infrastructural realities.

**Research Infrastructure**  
FreireVoice leverages the widespread accessibility of WhatsApp in Brazil and integrates a teacher-in-the-loop workflow for curating, reviewing, and governing learning packs. Educators retain oversight through lightweight dashboards and trigger-based intervention mechanisms, enabling human judgement at critical moments without undermining learner autonomy.


## Expected Outcomes

- A deployable FreireVoice prototype refined through iterative co-design and field validation.
- Empirical evidence on asynchronous, voice-based generative AI tutoring in low-literacy and low-connectivity contexts.
- A set of design principles and an open-source toolkit (dialogue protocols, co-design guides, and inclusive deployment manuals) for teacher-in-the-loop AI tutoring systems.
- A design framework for AI-powered education in low-resource contexts, articulating key dimensions of interaction modality, epistemic stance, governance granularity, and breakdown handling, grounded in a year-long co-design and field deployment in Brazil.
